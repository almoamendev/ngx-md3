Motion

md-motion.scss defines {{ total }} tokens — a duration and an easing curve for every combination of set, speed and kind. They always come in pairs: a duration is meaningless without the curve it was tuned against.

Spatial tokens are for anything that moves or changes size: transforms, width and height, position. Effects tokens are for anything that doesn't: opacity, color, shadow. The split exists because the two read very differently — spatial curves overshoot slightly to feel physical, while effects curves settle without overshoot, since a color that overshoots just looks like a glitch.

Sets

Expressive is the default the library's own components use. Its spatial curves are springier, with a pronounced overshoot that makes movement feel responsive. Standard is the more restrained alternative — same durations, curves that ease to their end value without bouncing past it. Use it where motion should stay out of the way, such as dense or data-heavy interfaces.

Speeds

Pick by the distance travelled, not by importance: fast for small movements within a component, default for most transitions, slow for large or full-screen changes. Effects durations are much shorter than spatial ones at every speed, because a fade needs far less time than a movement to read as deliberate.

The previews above loop continuously. Easing rows animate at a fixed rate so only the curve differs; duration rows animate linearly so only the timing differs. They are suppressed when the system requests reduced motion.
