Bottom sheets

Bottom sheets are panels anchored to the bottom of the viewport that hold supporting content or actions related to the current screen. A modal sheet floats above the page with a scrim, the same way a dialog does, and is the default. A standard sheet floats above the page without one: it docks into the scaffold above the bottom bar, leaves the rest of the page scrollable and clickable, and is dragged between a collapsed height and an expanded one.

  • Bottom sheets are custom components opened through the bottom sheet service, the same way dialogs are opened through the dialog service.
  • The sheet lays your component's content out on a grid with header, body and actions areas. Place your own elements in them with grid-area; the body is the part that scrolls.
  • Dragging a sheet moves it to the next height in the direction of travel, once the drag covers about 40% of the gap or is flung. A shorter drag snaps back. A modal sheet has one height and dragging it down dismisses it; a standard sheet moves between collapsed and expanded, and only closes on a drag when dismissible is on. The drag handle is the affordance, and is where the gesture is most reliable on touch, but the drag starts anywhere on the sheet that is not scrolling content of its own. Set gestures: false to turn it off.
  • A modal sheet brings its own scrim, blocks page scrolling, traps focus, and closes when the scrim is clicked, when Escape is pressed, or when another modal sheet opens on top of it.
  • A standard sheet does none of that: no scrim, no focus trap, Escape is left alone, and the page behind it keeps scrolling and taking clicks. It stays open until you close it. One of each kind may be open at the same time, and opening a sheet only replaces one of its own kind.
  • On a standard sheet the drag handle is also a button: it toggles the two heights on click, and responds to Enter, Space and the up and down arrows. Arrow down collapses but never dismisses, since there is no undo for that from the keyboard.
  • Give a standard sheet a label or a labelledBy. It renders as a labelled region, and an unnamed one is silently not exposed as a landmark.

Outlets

A modal sheet renders in an overlay of its own and works anywhere. A standard sheet renders into an outlet in the page scaffold, which is what anchors it to the layout rather than the viewport. The scaffold registers that outlet on its own, in a region between the content and the bottom bar, so a standard sheet opened anywhere in the app lands in the current layout and sits above a navigation bar rather than over it.

  • The region takes no space of its own. The sheet floats over the content, at the same maximum width as a modal sheet's panel, so opening or dragging one never reflows the page behind it.
  • Because it reserves nothing, a snackbar will overlap a standard sheet. Pass bottomOffset when opening one to clear it.
  • Calling open() with type: 'standard' and no registered outlet throws — the layout has to be in place first.
  • There is no live switch between the two types, unlike side sheets, which have a setSheetType. The two variants live in different hosts here, so changing type means closing the sheet and opening it again.
